## What contrast ratio actually measures

Every color has a **relative luminance** — how much light it reflects, on a 0 (black) to 1 (white) scale, computed from its red/green/blue channel values. Contrast ratio compares the relative luminance of two colors:

```
ratio = (lighter luminance + 0.05) / (darker luminance + 0.05)
```

Black-on-white luminance values (0 and 1) plug in to give exactly **21:1** — the maximum possible ratio. Two identical colors give exactly **1:1** — no contrast at all.

## WCAG AA thresholds

| Content | Minimum ratio |
|---|---|
| Normal text | 4.5:1 |
| Large text (18pt+, or 14pt+ bold) | 3:1 |
| UI components & graphical objects (input borders, icons) | 3:1 |

AAA, a stricter optional level, raises these to 7:1 and 4.5:1 respectively. Most products target AA — it's the level most accessibility laws and audits reference.

## Why large text gets a lower bar

Larger, heavier letterforms have more pixels making up each character, so they stay legible at a lower contrast ratio than small text does — the requirement scales with how much visual detail is actually needed to distinguish the shape.

## UI components need contrast too, not just text

WCAG 1.4.11 (Non-text Contrast) extends the 3:1 requirement to meaningful graphical elements: an input's border against its background, an icon that conveys information, a focus indicator. A form field with a near-invisible 1px border can fail this even if its label text passes the 4.5:1 text requirement.

## Never rely on color alone

WCAG 1.4.1 requires that information conveyed by color also be conveyed some other way — text, an icon, a pattern. A required-field asterisk, an icon next to an error message, or an underline on a link (not just a color change) all satisfy this. The common failure case: a form field that turns its border red on error with no icon, no text, and no other visual change — a colorblind user may not perceive that anything changed at all.

## Testing in practice

Browser DevTools' color picker (Chrome, Firefox) shows the computed contrast ratio for any two colors directly, flagging AA/AAA pass or fail — the same formula covered above, just computed for you.
